Research vision

More inclusive genetics. Better science. Better health.

Developing population-aware and molecular approaches that improve genetic discovery in global populations, with a particular focus on admixed populations, and move findings toward precision medicine.

Research at a glance

Connecting population diversity to molecular mechanisms and clinical discovery.

At the Montalvo-Ortiz lab, our research integrates population genetics, statistical genetics, genomics, epigenomics, transcriptomics, and multi-omics to understand the biology of psychiatric and substance-use phenotypes, with a particular focus on Latin American and other historically underrepresented populations.

About

Building genetics research across populations and biological scales.

I am a postdoctoral researcher in the Yale Department of Psychiatry, where I work at the intersection of human genetics, population genetics, and molecular psychiatry.

Our work focuses on genetic discovery in admixed populations, cross-ancestry analysis, genome-wide association studies, polygenic scores, local ancestry, electronic health record phenotypes, and multi-omic approaches to psychiatric disease and substance use.

A central goal of our research is to develop and apply approaches that better represent genetic diversity across Latin America and other underrepresented populations. I am also interested in translating genetic discoveries into pharmacogenomics and precision psychiatry.

I have contributed to collaborative work spanning large biobanks, international consortia, postmortem brain tissue, sequencing studies, and multi-omic datasets.

Selected work

Research projects

LAGC

Latin American Genomics Consortium

Collaborative genetics across Latin American populations, including large-scale alcohol and smoking GWAS, ancestry-aware analyses, polygenic prediction, and multi-omic integration.

ADDICTION

Alcohol & Substance-Use Genetics

Genetic architecture of alcohol use and problematic drinking across diverse cohorts, with emphasis on ancestry diversity, structural variation, and molecular mechanisms.

OUD

Opioid Use Disorder Multi-Omics

Cell-type-resolved chromatin accessibility and gene expression together with base-resolution DNA methylation in human postmortem orbitofrontal cortex to study regulatory mechanisms of OUD, particularly in glial populations.

SV GENETICS

Structural Variants in Addiction

Long-read structural-variant reference resources and imputation approaches to investigate genetic variation that is difficult to capture with conventional SNP arrays.

AGING

Transcriptomic Aging

Comparing RNA-based biological age prediction approaches and machine-learning models in postmortem human prefrontal cortex.

AI + BIOMEDICINE

Evidence Intelligence Platform

A computational framework for retrieving, classifying, extracting, quality-controlling, and querying biomedical literature at scale.
